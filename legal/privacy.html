In short

  • What you write in LIFE/OS is saved on your own device, in your browser. We never receive it, so we can't read it, sell it or share it.
  • There is no account, no analytics, no ads, no tracking and no cookies.
  • Some of what you can keep is sensitive, such as the days you relapsed on the Recovery page, which can say something about your health. It is kept the same way: on your device, and only with Sync in your own repository.
  • Sync is optional. It copies your data to a private repository in your own GitHub account (Sync only works with a private one), with a key you create. It goes straight from your browser to GitHub; we don't see it.
  • The website is hosted by GitHub. Like any web host, GitHub's servers see your IP address when your browser loads the app, and GitHub logs it for security.
  • You can download all your data or delete it at any time: how to delete your data.

2. How LIFE/OS works

LIFE/OS is a web app. Your browser downloads the app's files (its pages, styles, code, typefaces and pictures) from the website and runs them on your device. Everything you enter is then saved by your browser, on your device: your tasks, notes, habits and ticks, calendar events, focus timer sessions, workouts, the subscriptions and daily results you track under Finance, what you track under Recovery, and your settings.

LIFE/OS has no server and no database of its own, and no account. We have no way to see what is stored in your browser, and we never receive it.

3.1 What you enter is saved on your device

The app running in your browser is the only thing that reads or writes your data. It is kept in your browser's local storage for the website's address; the cookies and storage page lists exactly what is stored and why. Using LIFE/OS for your own life is a purely personal activity, which the GDPR does not cover (article 2(2)(c)), and since your data never reaches us, we cannot read, change, lose, recover or hand it over.

Finance keeps the subscriptions you add (name, price, when you pay) and, only if you turn on Daily results, the profit or loss you note for each day on each account you keep there (a business or trading, for example). LIFE/OS doesn't connect to any bank, shop or broker: it only knows the numbers you type in.

Recovery keeps what you choose to stay away from (smoking or alcohol, for example), the day you started and the days you marked as a relapse. That can say something about your health, which the GDPR treats as a special category of personal data (article 9). It is kept exactly like the rest: in this browser on your device, and only if you turn on Sync, in your own private GitHub repository (3.2). We never receive it, nothing about it is worked out anywhere but on your device, and we don't use it for anything. Keep your device locked and your repository private.

Pictures you add to a task's page are kept in the same browser, in its database for the website's address (IndexedDB), made smaller first (at most 1600 pixels on the longest side). They stay on the device you added them on: Sync never sends them, so on your other devices the page only says a picture is there, and backup files don't include them.

Download a backup (in Settings) saves one file with all your data on your device; the sync key is never included. Restore a backup first keeps a copy of what was there, in your browser.

3.2 Sync, if you turn it on

Sync is off until you connect it. To connect, you make a repository called life-os-data in your GitHub account (it must be private: anyone can read a public one), create a key on GitHub's website, set to read and write only that repository, and paste the key into LIFE/OS. From then on, the app on your device sends your tasks, notes, habits, calendar, focus timer, training, finance and recovery data to that repository through GitHub's API (api.github.com), and fetches changes made on your other computers. Appearance, what is on screen and the key itself are not synced. GitHub keeps every sync as a version, named after the part of the app and the kind of device it came from (for example "Notes · Mac").

Sync only works with a private repository. It checks when you connect, at the first sync in each window and then once an hour. If the repository isn't private, Sync stops there and sends nothing to it: make it private, then try again. A repository made public shows anyone what is already in it, history included, so keep it private.

This happens under your own agreement with GitHub (GitHub's Terms of Service and Privacy Statement). We are not part of it and receive nothing. You can turn Sync off at any time in the Sync dialog; your data then stays on your device and in your repository until you delete it. If a sync is running when you click Turn off sync (or Delete all my data), it stops there: nothing more is uploaded to GitHub, nothing more from GitHub is saved on your device, and Sync's record of the last sync is not written back.

3.3 The website and its host

The LIFE/OS website is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc. (USA). When your browser loads the app's files (on a visit, and when the app checks for a newer version), GitHub's servers necessarily receive your IP address and ordinary request details, such as the time, the file asked for and your browser's name and version. GitHub states that it logs and stores visitors' IP addresses for security purposes, whether or not the visitor has a GitHub account (About GitHub Pages).

We can't see these logs and we get no statistics about visitors. GitHub decides how it uses its own logs, as its Privacy Statement describes.

3.4 Typefaces, pictures and code: nothing from third parties

Everything the app shows comes from its own website: its typefaces, pictures and icons. Opening LIFE/OS loads nothing from Google, from advertising or analytics networks, or from any other third party, and the app runs no third-party code (no SDKs, plugins or trackers). The typefaces and some icon shapes come from open-source projects; they and their licences are listed on the credits and licenses page.

3.5 Notifications

If you turn on notifications for the focus timer, your browser asks for your permission first. The notifications are made by the app on your device: no push service or server is involved and nothing is sent anywhere. Turn them off in Settings or in your browser's site settings.

3.6 Links to other websites

A few buttons open GitHub's website: to create your life-os-data repository or a sync key, or to see your repository and its sync history. Links you write in your notes, and the website you can add to a subscription in Finance, open the same way. Each opens in a new tab only when you click it, without telling the site which page you came from, and that site's own privacy policy applies there.

8. How to delete your data

Because your data is on your device and in your own GitHub account, you can delete it yourself without asking anyone:

  1. Single items: delete them in the app (a task, note, habit, event, workout and so on).
  2. Everything on one device: in LIFE/OS open Settings → Sync & backup → Delete all my data. It lists what will go, offers a backup first and asks before it deletes. It removes everything LIFE/OS keeps in that browser (your data, the pictures in task pages, settings, safety backups, the sync key and the offline copy) and opens the app again empty.
  3. Or from the browser: clear the website's data.
    • Chrome and Edge: open LIFE/OS, click the icon left of the address, choose Site settings (or Cookies and site data) and Delete data.
    • Safari on a Mac: Settings → Privacy → Manage Website Data, search for the site's address, Remove.
    • Firefox: click the padlock left of the address, Clear cookies and site data.
    • An installed app (Chrome): uninstall it and tick Also delete data from Chrome.
    This erases your data and the app's offline copy in that browser. On the LIFE/OS website, the browser keeps the data of every site at the same github.io address together, so clearing it there also clears those other sites; the button in Settings removes only LIFE/OS's entries.
  4. Other devices and browsers: do the same on each one you used.
  5. Sync: click Turn off sync in the Sync dialog (this device forgets the key). To delete the synced data and all its history, delete the life-os-data repository on GitHub (the repository's Settings → Delete this repository), and delete the key at github.com/settings/personal-access-tokens.
  6. Backup files you downloaded: delete the files.
  7. Emails you sent us: ask us at [FILL IN: contact email] and we delete them (unless the law requires us to keep them), and confirm within one month.

Deleting cannot be undone. Download a backup first if you might want your data again.

9. Children

LIFE/OS is meant for people aged 13 or older. In Norway, 13 is the age from which a child can consent on their own to online services (personopplysningsloven § 5). Children under 13 should use LIFE/OS only with a parent's or guardian's permission. Sync needs a GitHub account, and GitHub only accepts users aged 13 or older.

LIFE/OS doesn't ask for anyone's age, name or contact details, and collects nothing about its users for us, so there is no child's data for us to hold. If you believe a child has sent us personal data by email, write to us and we delete it.

10. No automated decisions or profiling

We make no automated decisions about you and build no profiles (GDPR article 22). What the app works out, such as streaks, the LIFE index, the next payment of a subscription you track or your days clean, is calculated on your device, for you, and goes nowhere.

11. Security

  • Your data is as safe as your device: lock it with a password, and don't use LIFE/OS on a computer others can use under your login.
  • The website is served over HTTPS, and Sync talks to GitHub over HTTPS. Sync only works with a private repository, which only you, and anyone you give access to, can see (and GitHub, which stores it). Give no one else access to it.
  • Keep your sync key secret. If you think someone has it, delete it on GitHub and make a new one.
